EARL is SynthWorld's first synth: made with care and given freely, as a gift to music and to everyone who loves sound. It plays in your DAW as a CLAP, VST3 or LV2 plugin, or on its own as an application.

## Its sound

- **Three oscillators**, each one of four: a wavetable oscillator, three simple oscillators together, a sampler that plays your audio files, or a granular, made of grains of a sample, a wavetable or the sound coming in. Each has a phase and a frequency modulation input.
- **A noise**, the velvet noise or a coloured one, and **a sub-oscillator** under them.
- **Four filters**, each a ladder, a state variable, a comb or a formant filter.
- **A saturator and a bitcrusher on each voice**, so a chord through them is notes distorted one by one.

## Not all noise is white.

<!-- screenshot: velvet-noise -->

Velvet noise is silence with a pulse now and then, up or down at random. Most synths don't have it, and in EARL it's a sound of its own, one you play:

- **Thin the pulses out** and it crackles, one pulse at a time. **Thicken them** and it's a hiss, smoother than white noise.
- **Even them out** and it buzzes at a pitch. Turn on **KEY** and the pulses play your notes.
- **Widen them** and it grows darker and louder. Turn off **MONO** and each side of the stereo field gets a noise of its own.

</div>

## Rewire it

Every processor is a node in a graph you edit. Change the filters' order, split the sound's path and join it again, send a sound into a side chain.

<!-- screenshot: graph -->

## Its modulation

- On each voice, **four envelopes and six LFO slots**: each slot an LFO, a sample and hold, or a random drift, a slow wander for the life of analog gear.
- What you play moves things too: velocity, the key, aftertouch, MPE's timbre, the mod wheel, the pitch wheel, how a key is let go, a number drawn for each note, and where a voice is in its unison.
- **Eight macros**, for anything of the voices or of the effects.

<!-- screenshot: mod-matrix -->

## Its effects

**Eight effect slots** after the voices, each any of fourteen effects: filter, saturator, bitcrusher, chorus, flanger, phaser, delay, reverb, compressor, gate, limiter, parametric EQ, ring modulator and stereo width. Two envelopes, two LFO slots and an envelope follower are the effects' own. Turn on its audio input and EARL is an effect of whatever comes in, or plays it at your notes through a granular.

## Roll the dice

Out of ideas? Let EARL make a variation of your patch. **DEVIATION** sets how far it goes, from a nudge to a whole new sound, and you choose what may change: values, choices, modulation amounts, processors, cables, new modulations. What you've locked stays as it is.

Each roll fades in without a click and is one step to undo: roll again until it's the one.

## Two kinds of unison

<div class="versus-columns">
<div>

### On an oscillator

<!-- screenshot: oscillator-unison -->

Up to 16 copies of a wavetable oscillator, detuned, spread across the stereo field, each at its own place in the wavetable. Thick and wide, and light on the CPU.

</div>
<div>

### On the whole voice

<!-- screenshot: voice-unison -->

Up to 8 whole voices for each note, each with its own oscillators, filters, envelopes and LFOs. So they don't have to be copies:

- **Spread anything across them**: a cutoff, a wavetable's position, one oscillator's pitch.
- **Let each live its own way**: its own random drift, its own granular cloud.
- **Combine it** with the oscillators' unison.

</div>
</div>

## Its tunings

Play beyond twelve notes: scales of any number of notes, from EARL's own tunings or your Scala files, for microtonal and xenharmonic music. Even scales that don't repeat at the octave, like Bohlen-Pierce.

<!-- screenshot: tuning -->

## Its skins

Choose how it looks, and the whole window changes at once: SynthWorld's own skin, dark or light; **Deep Sea**, in navy blues; **Riso**, a riso-printed poster with shapes of its own, its knobs inked suns and its displays windows of straw paper; and on Linux, **Tux**. EARL asks which one you want the first time you open it, and you can change it in its settings whenever you like. The sound never changes.
